Daylight Wheel

Solar noon, the moment the sun is highest in the sky, is set by physics. The clock on the wall is a human choice. Turn the clock face to see how different choices line up with the sun through the year.

Drag the clock face to turn it. Hold Shift to snap to 15 minutes.

What you're looking at

The outer ring is the sun. One trip around the wheel is 24 hours, and solar noon is always at the top. Yellow is daylight, purple is twilight (the sun less than 6° below the horizon), and navy is night. Move the day slider to watch the days lengthen and shorten through the year.

The inner dial is a clock. How it is set is just a choice of offset from UTC, the world's reference time. Drag it to any setting you like. Align noon with the sun makes clock noon match the selected day's solar noon. The Standard and Daylight Saving buttons set it to the legal time where you are.

Why the clock drifts. Align the clock with the sun, then move the day slider: the clock creeps away from the top by up to about a quarter of an hour, then comes back. Because Earth's axis is tilted and its orbit is an ellipse, solar noon arrives a little earlier or later than average at different times of year (the equation of time). A clock that ticks at a steady rate can't follow the sun exactly.

Time zones and Daylight Saving time. Standard time is shared across a whole time zone, so for most places it already sits minutes, or even an hour or more, away from the sun. Daylight Saving time moves the clock another hour, shifting daylight from the morning to the evening. Try comparing sunrise and sunset in December and June under each setting, and see which you would choose.
